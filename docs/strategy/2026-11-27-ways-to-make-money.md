# Ways to make money, and whether a nonprofit would work

**Owner:** Martin · **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-27 · **Status:** Brainstorm for
Martin's review. The legal form is decided: an ehf. early in 2027 (A58,
[ADR 0053](../decisions/0053-a-private-limited-company-not-a-nonprofit.md))

> Not legal or tax advice. Facts below were checked on official Icelandic
> sources on 27 November 2026 (links at the end). Money figures marked
> **guess** are assumptions to argue with, not research. Items marked
> **verify** go to the accountant or the lawyer.

## In short

1. **Register an ehf. early in 2027, not a nonprofit.** A nonprofit is legal
   and Martin could be paid a normal salary in one, but PuffinRide would get
   almost none of the benefits people expect: no income tax exemption, no
   tax-deductible donations, a higher tax rate on any surplus (37.6% instead
   of 20%), no R&D tax credit, and Martin would share control with a board
   and own nothing.
2. **Grants and the R&D tax credit pay for 2027.** The fee cannot start
   before card payments (milestone 15), and the grants that fit PuffinRide
   close between January and March 2027. The R&D tax credit gives back 35% of development costs, so
   about 4 million kr on one developer's salary, but only to a company.
3. **The passenger fee alone will not pay a salary for years.** It needs
   about 164 seats a day at today's guesses. Keep it (ADR 0014), small and
   with a floor, as one stream of several.
4. **Employers, regions and events are the realistic second leg.** Liftshare
   in the UK is the model: the public service stays free and organisations
   pay for their own schemes. Start two or three employer pilots in 2027.
   A yearly estimate of the CO2 their staff saved helps sell it; selling
   carbon credits does not pay (2.14).
5. **Say no to** a cut from drivers, ads, selling data and paid boosts. They
   break the cost-sharing rules, the privacy promises or the trust the
   service needs, for little money.

## 1. Where PuffinRide starts from

example figures:

| --- | --- | --- |

| Running costs (hosting, texts, email, stores, tools) **guess** | 100 000 kr | 1 200 000 kr |
| **Total** | **1 042 800 kr** | **about 12.5 million kr** |

### The rules every idea has to pass

- **Taxi law.** Iceland's Taxi Act has no cost-sharing exemption. PuffinRide's
  case rests on drivers never earning more than their costs (rule P3 and the
  trip limit, ADR 0030). So money can never come from a cut of what drivers
  receive. Whether a fee makes PuffinRide a commercial dispatch station is
  already question 3 for the lawyer
  ([legal and regulatory](../compliance/legal-and-regulatory.md#9-questions-for-the-lawyer-phase-1-brief)).
- **No fee until card payments.** Passengers pay the driver in person until
  milestone 15 (ADR 0038), and ADR 0014 starts the fee with payments in the
  app.
- **Marketing never mentions payments or fees** (Martin, 2026-11-22).
- **Privacy.** No selling of personal data; anything shared is counted in
  totals ([privacy and GDPR](../compliance/privacy-gdpr.md)).
- **Free competition.** Shared rides in Iceland have long been free:
  Samferða (samferda.net) was a free site sponsored by Landvernd in 2006, and
  Facebook groups carry many lifts; "Skutlarar!" had 33 000 members in 2017,
  and the police called its paid lifts illegal in 2015. Whatever passengers
  pay must buy something they can see: reviews, a checked profile, refunds.

### What worked elsewhere

| Service | Form | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BlaBlaCar (France) | Company | Passengers pay a booking fee, added in 2012. Profitable since spring 2022; revenue about €195 m in 2022 and over €250 m in 2023. In some large markets (India, Brazil, Mexico, Turkey) it still charged nothing in 2024 and planned to add fees. |
| Liftshare (UK) | Company that calls itself a social enterprise | The public service is free. Employers, councils and transport hubs pay a set-up cost and a yearly licence for their own scheme (over 1 200 schemes and about £1 m income in 2012). |
| Skjutsgruppen (Sweden) | Nonprofit association since 2007, recognised as public-benefit | Grants, for example SEK 1.49 m from Vinnova's civil-society climate call (2022 to 2024). |
| France, public money | Policy | Local authorities pay drivers per passenger carried, for example €2 to €2.25 for the first 15 to 20 km, and the state matches local spending. Employers may give staff up to €900 a year tax-free for green commuting, carpooling included (forfait mobilités durables). The state's €100 new-driver bonus ran 2023 to 2024 and ended on 1 January 2025. |

The lesson: BlaBlaCar needed tens of millions of members before fees paid
the bills. In a country of under 400 000 people, organisations and public
money do the heavy lifting first.

### 2.1 Passenger service fee (ADR 0014)

Already decided for public launch. What it brings, after VAT and the card fee
that PuffinRide pays on the whole payment:

| Seat price | Fee at 10% | Left for PuffinRide at 24% VAT | At 11% VAT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 000 kr (a long ride) | 400 kr | 213 kr | 250 kr |
| 900 kr (Reykjavík to Selfoss) | 90 kr | 48 kr | 56 kr |

| Seats a day at 213 kr | Per year |
| --- | --- |
| 20 | 1.6 m kr |
| 50 | 3.9 m kr |
| 80 | 6.2 m kr |
| 164 | 12.8 m kr, the whole budget |

Points for rule P5, when the amount is set:

- **A floor** (for example 10% but at least 150 kr) so short rides carry
  their own card cost. On a 900 kr seat a flat percentage leaves almost
  nothing. **guess**
- **VAT rate (verify).** Passenger transport that is not public transport
  carries 11% VAT, and so does a travel agent's help in selling it. If the
  fee counts as that, PuffinRide keeps about 250 kr instead of 213 kr. If it
  counts as an ordinary online service, 24%. VAT registration is needed once
  taxable sales pass 2 000 000 kr in 12 months.
- Fewer, larger card payments save money: card fees on small amounts are
  the biggest cost after VAT.

### 2.2 Commuter pass

A monthly price instead of a fee on every booking, for people who ride the
same corridor every day. It rewards loyalty and smooths income, but ADR 0014
rejected a subscription as the main model because most riders are
occasional. Add it with recurring trips (phase 5), not before.

### 2.3 Employers: "PuffinRide for work"

The best fit for the second leg, and it needs no card payments.

- **What they get:** a private group for staff (checked by work email),
  trips to and from the workplace shown first, a monthly report of shared
  rides and car kilometres saved, and help launching it on site.
- **Who:** large workplaces that people commute to from another town. For
  example Keflavík Airport from the capital area, the smelters at
  Grundartangi and Reyðarfjörður, hospitals, universities, and workplaces
  in Selfoss, Akranes and Reykjanesbær.
- **Price:** a set-up fee and a yearly licence, as Liftshare charges.
  200 000 to 600 000 kr a year per workplace. **guess**
- **A catch:** Iceland's tax-free commuting allowance (samgöngugreiðslur, up
  to 138 000 kr a year in 2026) covers public transport, walking and
  cycling, but "not use of a car of any kind". So employers cannot reward
  carpooling tax-free today, as French employers can. That is worth raising
  with the government, alone or with employers, but do not plan on it.
- **Build cost:** private groups, work-email checks and reports are new
  features. Start with one pilot workplace by hand before building them.

### 2.4 Regions and municipalities

Rural towns lose bus routes, and regional bodies already fund projects that
keep people mobile. PuffinRide can offer a region a yearly package: meeting
points in every town, local promotion, and a report of trips in totals.

- Start with SASS (South Iceland) and the regions on the beta corridors.
  The South Iceland development fund is already in the grants list.
- France shows the bigger version: local authorities pay drivers per
  passenger. Iceland has no such scheme yet. Suggest it once there is data.
- 1 to 3 million kr per region a year. **guess**

### 2.5 Tourists and tourism partners

2.27 million foreign visitors left through Keflavík in 2025, and 92% came
on holiday. Many travel the Ring Road, where seat prices are higher, so they
bring more fee per booking than short commuter hops. Partners such as
hostels, campsites and car rentals can promote PuffinRide ("renting a car?
share your empty seats") in exchange for a mention. Expect promotion, not
much cash. Check that car rental terms allow cost-sharing passengers before
promoting it (**verify**).

### 2.6 Events and festivals

Festivals outside Reykjavík have the classic problem PuffinRide solves:
thousands of people going the same way on the same day. An "official ride
sharing partner" deal can include a fee, a page for the event and meeting
points near the site. 100 000 to 500 000 kr per event. **guess** Try one in
summer 2027.

### 2.7 Local sponsors at meeting points

Meeting points are often at a petrol station, a shop or a café that gains
visitors. A sponsor could pay to be named on the point ("Meeting point at
the café"). Small money, and only if Martin keeps choosing the points:
a sponsor must never decide where points are.

### 2.8 Perks from fuel, charging or insurance companies

Fuel and charging discounts for drivers, or a trip insurance offer, make
driving with PuffinRide more attractive. Companies may pay a little for the
reach, but treat this as a perk for drivers, not income. Any data shared
with a partner needs consent ([privacy and GDPR](../compliance/privacy-gdpr.md)).

### 2.9 Grants and the R&D tax credit

The full list, with deadlines, is in the grants report of 22 November 2026
(project files, `grants/puffinride-grants-2026-11.md`). For 2027:

| What | Amount | Needs the ehf.? | Next date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vegagerðin research fund | Not stated | No | Expected January 2027 (2026: 30 January) |
| NORA | Up to DKK 500 000 a year | No; needs a partner in the Faroe Islands, Greenland or coastal Norway | 8 March 2027 |
| Lóa, rural innovation grants | Up to about 20 m kr, 30% co-funding | No | Expected end of March 2027 |
| Nýsköpunarsjóður (the new Innovation Fund, Act 54/2026) | Sproti was up to 12 m kr a year for 2 years | Yes, before the contract | Categories published January 2027 |
| South Iceland development fund (SASS) | Up to 2.5 m kr | No | Next round not announced |
| R&D tax credit (Act 152/2009) | 35% of approved R&D costs, paid in cash when there is no tax to reduce | **Yes** | Apply to Rannís by 1 October 2027 for 2027 costs |

The R&D tax credit matters most. On one developer's yearly cost of about
11.3 m kr, 35% is about 4 m kr. It is paid the year after, when the
company's tax is assessed, and only if Rannís accepts the work as research
or development (**verify**: routine software work may not count).

### 2.10 Donations

A "support PuffinRide" button costs little to add, but donations to an ehf.
are not tax-deductible, people give little to a company, and it sits close
to "payments" in what the app says. Skip it unless PuffinRide becomes a
nonprofit.

### 2.11 to 2.13 What not to do

- **Ads in the app.** The audience is small, ads need consent (Martin already
  chose no Meta Pixel), and they cheapen a service that runs on trust.
- **Selling data.** Against the privacy promises. A report of trips in totals
  can be part of a research grant or a region's package, never personal
  data.
- **A cut from drivers, or paid boosts for drivers.** Both take from what
  drivers receive or let money decide who gets passengers. They weaken the
  cost-sharing case under the Taxi Act and break ADR 0014.

### 2.14 CO2: count it, do not sell it

Martin asked on 27 November 2026 whether the CO2 that shared rides save
could earn money. Not directly, for now. Counted honestly, though, it makes
options 3, 4 and 9 easier to sell.

#### Selling carbon credits: no, for now

- A crediting method for exactly this exists. The Global Carbon Council's
  GCCM007 (2025) covers long-distance carpooling between towns on
  cost-sharing platforms whose drivers make no profit. Verra, the largest
  standard, rejected a carpooling method on 30 January 2024 because it saves
  little and it is hard to prove the rides would not have happened anyway.
- In Iceland the saving is already counted. Less fuel sold shows up in
  Iceland's own emissions inventory, where road transport is under the
  effort-sharing target (samfélagslosun). A credit sold abroad would count
  the same tonne twice unless the government authorised it and adjusted its
  own accounts. The government's working group on carbon markets
  (November 2024) advised against exports that harm Iceland's accounts.
- It would be small. At the 164 seats a day the salary needs (section 1),
  about 150 km a seat and about 0.16 kg CO2 per car kilometre (a petrol car
  using 7 litres per 100 km), the most PuffinRide could claim is about
  1 400 tonnes a year, and only if every passenger would otherwise have
  driven alone. Many would have taken the bus or not travelled, so a few
  hundred tonnes is more likely. Even at a generous 10 euros a tonne,
  1 400 tonnes is about 2 million kr a year and a few hundred tonnes well
  under 1 million kr, before paying independent auditors to approve the
  project and check every issue of credits. **guess**

#### Where CO2 does help

- **Employers and public bodies.** Iceland's Climate Act (70/2012) requires
  state bodies, state-owned companies and municipalities to adopt climate
  policies with emission-reduction goals. Many private firms also publish
  climate accounts, in which staff commuting is a standard category (Scope 3,
  category 7 of the Greenhouse Gas Protocol). A yearly figure of car
  kilometres and CO2 saved by their own staff is something they can put
  straight into those. Add it to the "PuffinRide for work" report (2.3) and
  the regional package (2.4). EU reporting rules will not make anyone ask:
  since the 2025 changes, the sustainability reporting directive (CSRD)
  covers only companies with more than 1 000 staff and more than 450 million
  euros turnover, and Iceland was still consulting on its own law in 2024
  (S-159/2024).
- **Grants.** A measured saving strengthens every application in 2.9. The
  Climate and Energy Fund (Loftslags- og orkusjóður) needs a legal entity
  and favours hardware; its December 2026 call is for greenhouses only. The
  EU's LIFE programme has September deadlines and advisers at Rannís, but it
  suits a larger project with partners. Later, not 2027.
- **Members.** A line such as "about 60 kg CO2 saved" for a passenger who
  would otherwise have driven alone from Reykjavík to Akureyri, or a yearly
  total, is a nice touch. Green claims need care: from 27 September 2026 the
  EU bans vague environmental claims and claims of being climate neutral
  through offsets (Directive 2024/825). Whether that applies in Iceland
  through the EEA yet is not checked (**verify**), and Iceland's marketing
  law already bans misleading claims. Say "estimated", show the method, and
  never say "carbon neutral". This changes the product, so it needs its own
  spec: [spec 34](../specs/2026-11-28-co2-saved.md) (2026-11-28).

To make the figure honest, PuffinRide would need to know how passengers
would have travelled otherwise. One optional question after a trip, reported
only in totals, would do it ([privacy and GDPR](../compliance/privacy-gdpr.md)).

## 3. Would a nonprofit work?

Short answer: **legally yes, but it brings far less than people expect, and
it costs Martin ownership and control.**

### 3.1 The legal forms

| Form | Founders | Money needed | Registration fee | May it run a business? | Who controls | Tax on profit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ehf. (private limited company, Act 138/1994) | 1 is enough | 500 000 kr share capital, paid in | 140 500 kr | Yes | The owner | 20% |
| Félag til almannaheilla (public benefit association, Act 110/2021) | At least 3 sign the founding agreement | None | 30 000 kr | Yes, if named in its statutes or derived from its purpose | Members at the yearly meeting elect a board of at least 3 | 37.6% |
| Almenn félagasamtök (general association) | 2 or more | None | 30 000 kr | Not meant for it (Skatturinn) | Members | 37.6% if it does |
| Sjálfseignarstofnun í atvinnurekstri (ses., self-owning institution in business, Act 33/1999) | Founders give money irrevocably | At least 1 000 000 kr, paid in | 140 500 kr | Yes | A board of at least 3 that picks its own successors unless the statutes say otherwise | 37.6% |
| Foundation with a confirmed charter (Act 19/1988) | Founders | Set in the charter | Confirmed by the district commissioner in the Westfjords | No; business moves it under Act 33/1999 | Board | Exempt if no business |
| Samvinnufélag (co-operative, Act 22/1991) | At least 3 at a founding meeting | No fixed amount | 276 500 kr | Yes, for members' benefit | Members | 20% |
| Sole trader (Martin now) | 1 | None | n/a | Yes | Martin | Personal income tax, up to 46.29% in 2026 |

A co-operative of drivers and passengers is an interesting idea, but it is
run for its members' economic benefit, is not a nonprofit, and needs many
active members. It is not a fit for a one-person start.

### 3.2 Tax: where the nonprofit idea mostly falls down

- **Income tax.** Associations and self-owning institutions pay 37.6% on
  taxable profit, against 20% for an ehf. (Income Tax Act 90/2003, article
  71, with article 2, item 5). They are exempt only if they run no business
  at all (article 4, item 5), or if they use their profit only for public
  benefit as the law defines it (article 4, item 4). That definition is a
  closed list: humanitarian and charity work; youth and culture; rescue
  teams; scientific research; university and education funds; consumer and
  prevention work; churches and registered belief societies. **Transport,
  the environment and regional development are not on it**, and a platform
  that charges fees runs a business.
- **Tax-deductible donations.** People can deduct 10 000 to 350 000 kr a year
  given to organisations on Skatturinn's public benefit register
  (almannaheillaskrá), and companies up to 1.5% of turnover. To be on it, an
  organisation must be non-profit, socially important, built largely on
  volunteers' work, work in one of the listed areas, and run no business
  beyond small fundraising. PuffinRide would very likely not qualify.
  Being in the separate register of public benefit associations under Act
  110/2021 does not put an organisation on the tax register automatically.
- **VAT** is the same as for a company: organisations that sell taxable
  services in competition with businesses charge VAT.
- **R&D tax credit.** Act 152/2009 covers only companies (article 2, items 1
  and 3 of the Income Tax Act: hf., ehf. and partnerships). An association,
  a self-owning institution or a co-operative cannot claim it. That alone is
  worth about 4 m kr a year to PuffinRide while it is being built.

Most of the time a nonprofit PuffinRide would aim to spend what it earns on
salaries and costs, so there would be little profit to tax at either rate.
The real tax loss is the R&D credit, and the hoped-for tax wins
(exemption and deductible gifts) are not available.

### 3.3 Grants: what a nonprofit opens and closes

- **Opens:** public bodies may require registration as a public benefit
  association for their grants and operating contracts (Act 110/2021,
  article 2), so some public money prefers nonprofits. Civil-society calls,
  such as the one that funded Skjutsgruppen, are open to it. In some EU
  programmes a private company cannot lead a project (Interreg's Northern
  Periphery and Arctic programme, per the grants report); whether a
  nonprofit could is worth checking. Company
  community funds (Landsvirkjun, Landsbankinn) give to charity, culture,
  education and sport; mobility is not their usual theme.
- **Closes:** the R&D tax credit (above). The new Nýsköpunarsjóður funds
  "entrepreneurs and small and medium-sized companies"; whether an
  association counts is unclear (**verify** with Rannís).
- **No difference:** Lóa, SASS, Vegagerðin's research fund and NORA accept
  individuals as well as organisations, so the form barely matters there.

### 3.4 Could Martin still be paid?

Yes. Both laws let the board hire a managing director (framkvæmdastjóri)
and pay a salary, as long as no one is paid "more than is normal given the
nature and scope of the work" (Act 110/2021 article 21; Act 33/1999 articles
20 and 34). A market salary for a full-time developer and manager very
likely counts as normal. What changes:

- **No profit, no ownership.** Martin cannot take dividends, sell the
  organisation or raise money from investors. If it closes, its assets go to
  a similar public benefit cause, not to Martin.
- **A board decides.** The board, not Martin alone, sets Martin's pay and
  can replace Martin. In an association, members elect the board at the yearly
  meeting, and anyone who meets the conditions in the statutes may join
  (Act 110/2021 article 6). In a ses., the managing director cannot chair the
  board, and people who are not managing directors must hold a majority of
  its seats (Act 33/1999 article 14). The minister must be told if the
  founder and the founder's family hold a majority (article 18).
- **More admin.** At least three people on a board, a yearly meeting, an
  auditor or inspector chosen by members, and accounts published on the
  website within a month of approval (Act 110/2021 article 22).

### 3.5 Does a nonprofit help with the taxi law?

Not by itself. A fee is business whatever the legal form (a ses. that sells
services counts as "in business" under Act 33/1999 article 3), and the
Taxi Act looks at what drivers receive. The cleanest legal position is a
PuffinRide that takes no fee from rides and lives on grants, employers and
sponsors, and an ehf. can do that too. Put this to the lawyer with question
3.

### 3.6 Verdict

| | Ehf. | Nonprofit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Martin's salary | Yes | Yes, "normal" pay set by a board |
| Martin owns it, can sell it or bring in investors | Yes | No |
| Tax on profit | 20% | 37.6% (no exemption for transport) |
| Tax-deductible donations | No | Very likely no |
| R&D tax credit (35%) | Yes | No |
| Grants | Nearly all | A few more public and civil-society ones, maybe not Nýsköpunarsjóður |
| Needed for card payments and the store accounts | Yes | Also possible |
| People needed to start | Martin | At least 3 founders and a board |
| Start-up money | 640 500 kr (500 000 of it stays in the company) | 30 000 kr (association) or 1 140 500 kr (ses.) |
| Trust and story | Can write its purpose into its statutes and publish its numbers | Built in |

**Recommendation: an ehf.** Register it early in 2027 so that it can sign the
Nýsköpunarsjóður contract, claim the R&D tax credit for 2027 and take over
the store accounts (ADR 0051) and, later, card payments.

To keep the trust a nonprofit would give, write into the ehf.'s statutes
that its purpose is cost-sharing travel, that it never takes a cut from
drivers, and publish a short yearly summary of rides and money. Liftshare
is a company and still calls itself a social enterprise.

A nonprofit makes sense only if Martin decides PuffinRide should stay free
for good and live on grants, sponsors and volunteers, as Skjutsgruppen does.
If that day comes, a separate public benefit association can be set up next
to the ehf. for community projects, with care for conflicts of interest
(Act 110/2021 article 21).
